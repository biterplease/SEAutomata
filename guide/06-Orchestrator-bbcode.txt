[h1]Ochestrator[/h1]

The Orchestrator works behind the scenes to ensure that blocks communicate effectively, and optimizes job plans for efficiency.

As the player, you will interact little with the Orchestrator, but it performs several key tasks that keep your system working effectively.

A single orchestrator is usually enough for an entire network. Multiple orchestrators in 1 network always defer to the oldest orchestrator present. If you sever the network link, each Orhcestrator will manage its own network, until re-linked.

The following is more about how the Automata Mod does things, rather than critical information for you to know. Feel free to skip to another section, or read on!

[h2]Jobs and Tasks[/h2]

[b]Jobs[/b] are the type of things that, as a player, you would do in order to, well, get other things done, like:

[list]
[*]Mining ore
[*]Get the ore to the refineries
[*]Take the ingots from the to the assembler
[*]Make components out of ingots
[*]Make spaceships out of components
[*]Destroy enemies with your spaceships
[/list]

While simple for an engineer to do, its awfully complicated for a drone to perform these jobs.

The orchestrator breaks down [b]jobs[/b] into simpler, single-goal [b]tasks[/b], that the drones CAN perform.

Tasks are usually very focused events that can be sequenced, to achieve jobs like a human can. For example, when given a job to mine Silicon, the orchestratror gives the drone a task list that looks like the following:

[list]
[*]Check inventory
[*]Empty inventory if not empty
[*]Undock
[*]Navigate to ore hotspot
[*]Use ore detector
[*]Approach desired ore
[*]Point drills towards ore
[*]Set dig mode
[*]Navigate to location (within 7.5m of detected ore)
[*]Switch to drill mode
[*]Drill
[/list]

After the drone starts drilling, several completion triggers tell the drone to stop. These usually come with task lists of their own:

[list]
[*]Completion trigger: cargo full -> Go to delivery point(s), and drop ore
[*]Completion trigger: no more ore (ore voxels no longer detected) -> return home, let Mining Surveyor deposit is depleted
[*]Abort trigger: low battery -> battery below threshold, go recharge
[/list]

The task lists a drone takes off with often range in the order of 100s, which encompass several trips of actions.

This job->task breakdown happens thanks to the Orchestrator.

[h2]Auctions[/h2]

[b]Auctions[/b] are how jobs are assigned.

The [b]Orchestrator[/b] uses an auction system to distribute tasks. Every job managed by an Orchestrator goes through the same four steps:

[olist]
[*][b]Announcement[/b]: a computer (Construction, Logistics, Mining Surveyor, or Target Designator) finds work and announces it. Some examples
[list]
[*]"These 10 blocks need welding"
[*]"This grid needs 500 steel plates"
[*]"The player placed an order for 10,000 kg of iron here"
[*]"This grid is requesting ice."
[/list]
[*][b]Auction[/b]: the Orchestrator opens an auction for the job and broadcasts it to every drone and Logistics Computer in range.
[*][b]Bidding[/b]:
[list]
[*]Drones that can do the job bid with their position, cargo room and tools.
[*]Logistics Computers that can supply the items (or take them) bid too.
[*]Most jobs need both: a drone to do the work, and a Logistics Computer to load from or unload to.
[/list]
[*][b]Winner selection[/b]: after a few seconds the Orchestrator picks the best bids.
[list]
[*]Drones whose behaviour profile matches the job always rank first, then the closest and best equipped.
[*]Big jobs are split over several drones and trips.
[*]The winners get their task lists, the Logistics Computers reserve the items or room, and the announcer stops repeating the job.
[*]Some jobs involve more than 1 winning drone or logistic computer:
[list]
[*]Example 1: large block weld. Welding a refinery, is 1 job that gets distributed to several drones, because of the large amount of components required
[*]Example 2: Mining byproducts: a grid with refineries would take unwanted stone, while a grid with O2/H2 gens would take unwanted ice
[/list]
[/list]
[/olist]

If nobody bids, the job is put on hold for a few minutes and announced again later. Every computer has a [b]Retry jobs[/b] button to skip the wait.

[h2]Flight lanes[/h2]

[b]Flight lanes[/b] are like an air (or space) highway, to keep your drones following certain rules, for their own safety.

The orchestrator plans, and maintains, [b]Flight lanes[/b] to/from important locations:

[list]
[*]Between static bases: the Orchestrator automatically prepares flight lanes between all your static grids, ensuring Drones don't encounter each other head-on in transit.
[*]Between a base and an ore hotspot: The path that Mining Drones follow to/from a hotspot is always a flight lane
[list]
[*]A hotspot is when 2 or more ore deposits are within 500m
[*]Asteroids are always considered a hotspot
[/list]
[/list]

Flight lanes come in pairs, one for each direction. Each lane also includes sub-lanes, allowing slow drones to pass faster drones.

If a drone cannot fit the 40x40 flight lane, it will fly alongside of it.

You can visualize lanes using the command [i]/automata debugdraw lanes[/i]

[h2]Work areas[/h2]

The orchestrator assigns areas where several drones are operating as temporary work areas. In these areas, drones will act with extra care, and follow certain safety protocols.

Usually, drones are aware of their environment (provided they have a sensor and camera), but in work areas, they understand that other drones will be around.

In a work area, you'll notice a drone take pause, let another drone move or finish what its doing, and then resume once its path is clear

[h2]Parking protocol[/h2]

Drones with landing gears will park, according to the Orchestrator's setting

[list]
[*]Park on grid: anywhere this orchestrator's grid.
[*]Parking area: in the area set below, configure a space to do so..
[*]Voxel: on rock near this orchestrator.
[/list]

If [b]Parking protocol[/b] is unset, drones will figure it out themselves, usually preferring grid to voxel.
